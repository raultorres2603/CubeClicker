# Spec — Cube Visual Polish Follow-up (backdrop coverage, intro approach, level arrival)

## Summary

The user ran the manual visual pass over the accepted `cube-visual-polish` feature and reported three defects, all local to the frontend's rendering and phase timing: (1) the galaxy **backdrop is broken — the right side of the screen shows no stars**; (2) the **intro is broken — the camera should start far away *pointing at the cube* and approach it, instead of the current search/find choreography that keeps the cube off-screen**; (3) the **level transition is broken — the next cube must not appear instantly; the player should *go to* the next cube**. Each item is resolved below into binary-testable requirements with concrete pinned values. The fix for (1) replaces the bounded star *disk* (which the slow y-axis drift rotates until its projected extent no longer spans the viewport — the reported right-side gap) with a camera-anchored *cone* volume that provably covers the frustum at every pinned camera pose and supported aspect ratio, and turns the drift into a view-axis swirl whose coverage guarantee holds at every swirl angle — the volume's cross-section is a square whose inradius exceeds the largest supported viewport corner radius. The fix for (2) replaces the search→find→approach choreography with a straight, eased dolly-in that keeps the cube centred and fully framed from the first frame — reinstating the original accepted `cube-game-frontend` AC-22 intro values `(0,0,60) → (0,0,18)@0.5 → (0,0,12)`. The fix for (3) keeps the existing `travel` phase (no new phase): the departure (pull-away + backdrop expansion) runs for the first half, the next cube comes into existence at the turn-around distance, the camera flies back in during the second half, and `playing` begins exactly when the camera and the backdrop land on their rest values — no pop at the rest pose.

This feature supersedes named accepted behaviors by AC ID (see § Supersessions); everything else in the two accepted features stays in force. The predecessor specs (`docs/features/cube-game-frontend/spec.md` r2, `docs/features/cube-visual-polish/spec.md` r1) are authoritative for everything not superseded and are never edited by this feature.

## UX behavior (pinned)

### 1. Backdrop: camera-anchored cone volume + view-axis swirl (supersedes polish AC-1 mechanics and predecessor AC-3 positions)

**Diagnosis (root cause of "no stars on the right side").** The landed starfield is a bounded disk at the origin (`x ∈ [-70, 70]`, `y ∈ [-40, 40]`, `z ∈ [-60, -8]`) that the slow drift rotates rigidly about the **world y-axis** (`points.rotation.y += dtMs · 1/65536`). Rotating the disk about y swings its far z-span into x and its near right edge away from the camera: at drift angle θ ≈ 30° (≈ 33 s in) the projected right boundary of the disk recedes inside the frustum and the right side of the screen loses its near/mid stars; by θ ≈ 45° the entire right portion of the viewport is starless. Independently, at ultrawide aspects (≥ 21:9) even the unrotated disk runs out of depth at the frame edge. The defect is therefore **a distribution that does not cover the visible frame once the rigid yaw drift rotates it** (the last of the suspected mechanisms — not the x/y expansion scaling, which is symmetric about the screen centre, and not frustum culling, whose bounding sphere always intersects the frustum).

**Fix (pinned mechanism).** The starfield becomes a **camera-anchored, view-axis cone volume**:

- **Distribution.** Each star is drawn from the seeded random in the pinned order `x → y → depth → brightness` (4 draws per star, unchanged count `STAR_COUNT` = 1200). With `depth = STARFIELD_DEPTH_NEAR + draw · (STARFIELD_DEPTH_FAR − STARFIELD_DEPTH_NEAR)` (uniform in `[20, 160]`), the local position is `x = (draw − 0.5) · 2 · depth · STARFIELD_TAN`, `y = (draw − 0.5) · 2 · depth · STARFIELD_TAN`, `z = −depth`. The volume is a **square cone** — one half-angle tangent for both axes: `STARFIELD_TAN = tan(25°) · STARFIELD_ASPECT_MAX · (1 + STARFIELD_COVERAGE_MARGIN) = 1.251258882715913`, with `STARFIELD_ASPECT_MAX = 7/3` (21:9 ultrawide) and `STARFIELD_COVERAGE_MARGIN = 0.15`. The square's inradius `STARFIELD_TAN` exceeds the largest supported viewport corner radius (circumradius `√((tan(25°)·7/3)² + tan²(25°)) = 1.1840` at aspect 7/3), so the cross-section contains the frustum cross-section at every depth *for every swirl angle* — a square rotated by any angle spans at least its inradius in every direction. Because the distribution is uniform over the square (in slope space), the on-screen star density is constant over depth and aspect — ≈ 296 stars visible at 16:9 at rest scale, enough for the pinned per-cell floors (≥ 5 of ≈ 18.5 expected per 4×4 cell) at every sustained pose.
- **Anchor.** On every `update(dtMs)`, the starfield's `Points` is re-anchored to the camera: `position = camera.position`, `quaternion = camera.quaternion × RotZ(swirlAngle)`, where `swirlAngle += max(0, dtMs) · DRIFT_RADIANS_PER_MS` (rate retained `1/65536` rad/ms ≈ 0.0153 rad/s). The swirl rotates the square cone about the **view axis**. A rectangle is invariant only under 90° rotations, but a **square** rotated by any angle spans at least its inradius in every direction, and `STARFIELD_TAN` (the inradius) exceeds every supported viewport circumradius (`1.1840` at 7/3) — so the coverage guarantee holds at *every* swirl angle and can never degrade over time (the defect class is eliminated; the swirl stays the unbounded ambient drift at the retained rate). The anchor makes the volume's cross-section span the frustum at every depth in every phase: the camera always looks along −z from a position on the +z axis (rest, intro dolly, travel path), so the cone axis is the view axis by construction.
- **Expansion (retained intent).** The travel input still drives the uniform in-plane scale `(S, S, 1)` about the anchor (= the screen centre): stars move strictly outward for the whole expansion, and since `S ≥ 1` at every progress, the scaled cone still contains the frustum cross-section at every depth — the backdrop provably covers the viewport **at the maximum expansion** (AC-1's coverage test includes the scale-8 turn-around pose). z stays 1: every star stays inside the camera far plane (`depth ≤ 160 < 200`).
- **Reduced motion.** The expansion collapse is unchanged: the scene holds the travel input at 0, so the scale stays `(1,1,1)` at every travel progress. The anchor still follows the camera (which stays at rest). The swirl continues at the same rate — it is the predecessor's ambient drift (which was never collapsed under reduced motion), now around the view axis; stated explicitly.
- **Resources/determinism.** One `Points`, `STAR_COUNT` stars, 4 draws per star, seeded, never rebuilt across frames or phases (same object identity); the anchor reuses the caller's `position`/`quaternion` references and one preallocated swirl quaternion — zero per-frame allocation. AC-16 ceilings hold unchanged.

**Pinned constants** (in `render/starfield.ts`, documented as the frustum-coverage contract; the coverage tests below verify them against the real camera, so a divergence fails the build): `STARFIELD_DEPTH_NEAR = 20`, `STARFIELD_DEPTH_FAR = 160`, `STARFIELD_ASPECT_MAX = 7/3`, `STARFIELD_COVERAGE_MARGIN = 0.15`, `STARFIELD_TAN = Math.tan(Math.PI · 50 / 360) · (7/3) · 1.15 = 1.251258882715913` (the single square half-tan, used for **both** axes), `DRIFT_RADIANS_PER_MS = 1/65536` (retained). `STAR_SIZE` 0.6, brightness `[0.5, 1]`, material pins — unchanged.

### 2. Intro: straight dolly, pointing at the cube from the first frame (supersedes polish AC-2)

The intro phase keeps `INTRO_DURATION_MS` (2500), its skip semantics, and its reduced-motion collapse. The choreography is a pure function of `phaseProgress`:

- **Approach.** `introCameraPose(progress, outPosition, outTarget)` writes the target `(0, 0, 0)` for every clamped `progress` and the position `(0, 0, z)` with `z = 60 − 48 · easeOutCubic(clamp(progress))` — a straight, eased dolly-in from `INTRO_START_Z` (60) to the rest pose. The path is strictly monotonic (z strictly decreases; `easeOutCubic` is strictly increasing), and the camera looks exactly at the cube at every instant (quaternion identity at every p). `INTRO_START_Z = 60` reinstates the original accepted AC-22 intro start distance.

Pinned pose table (exact values asserted by node tests — all dyadic-exact doubles):

| p | camera position | lookAt target | quaternion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | `(0, 0, 60)` | `(0, 0, 0)` | identity |
| 0.25 | `(0, 0, 32.25)` | `(0, 0, 0)` | identity |
| 0.5 | `(0, 0, 18)` | `(0, 0, 0)` | identity |
| 0.75 | `(0, 0, 12.75)` | `(0, 0, 0)` | identity |
| 1 | `(0, 0, 12)` | `(0, 0, 0)` | identity |

**Framing guarantee.** For every pinned progress and every supported aspect ratio in `[2/3, 7/3]`, the cube (extent `CUBE_SIZE` box centred on the origin) projects **fully inside** the frustum: its centre is the screen centre (camera on the view axis, identity quaternion) and all eight corners pass `Frustum.containsPoint` — verified with the real camera at aspects 2/3, 16/9 and 7/3 at p ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1}. There are no off-screen frames: the cube is visible and framed from the very first frame. (The binding case is the closest pose at the narrowest aspect: corner half-angle `atan(2.5/9.5) ≈ 14.74°` vs the 2/3-aspect horizontal half-angle `atan(tan25°·2/3) ≈ 17.27°`.)

**Skip / reduced motion (retained).** A skip moves the phase to `playing`; the scene's `playing` branch puts the camera at rest immediately. Under reduced motion the camera stays at rest `(0,0,12)` at every p. The travel path and the `playing` rest branch are unchanged.

### 3. Level transition: depart, find the next cube at the turn-around, fly in (supersedes predecessor AC-10 timing and AC-22 travel values; keeps the `travel` phase)

**No new phase.** The whole transition remains inside the existing `travel` phase. `TRAVEL_DURATION_MS` stays 2500 (reduced 150).

- **First half — departure** (`p ∈ [0, 0.5]`): the camera pulls away from rest to the turn-around `(0, 0, 90)` and the backdrop expands from `(1,1,1)` to `(8,8,1)` — both eased with `easeInOutQuad` over the compressed input `2p`.
- **Midpoint — the next cube appears at a distance** (`p = 0.5`): the engine advances the level *exactly once* — `levelIndex +1`, fresh full board, next style in the pinned cycle, orientation reset to default, stats carried — and drains the one `levelChanged` intent while the camera is 90 units away at the turn-around. The new cube's body (extent 5 at distance 90, angular size ≈ 3.2°) comes into existence far down the view axis: no pop at the rest pose. The snapshot carries the new level's board/style/`levelIndex` from this moment; the HUD's `Cube N` advances during the fly-in.
- **Second half — arrival** (`p ∈ (0.5, 1]`): the camera flies back in from `(0, 0, 90)` to the exact rest pose `(0, 0, 12)`, and the backdrop contracts from `(8,8,1)` back to `(1,1,1)` — both with `easeInOutQuad` over `2p − 1`, mirroring the departure. At `p = 1` the phase becomes `playing` with the camera and the backdrop exactly at their rest values — the transition ends in a continuous landing, never a snap.
- **Input stays gated** for the whole `travel` (engine contract unchanged); `travelStarted` still drains once at travel start.

The shared shape is one pure function in `game/effects.ts`:

```ts
/** TRAVEL_LEVEL_ADVANCE_P = 0.5 — travel-progress fraction at which the level advances. */
export const TRAVEL_LEVEL_ADVANCE_P = 0.5;
/** Symmetric swing: easeInOutQuad(2p) for p ≤ 0.5, 1 − easeInOutQuad(2p − 1) after;
 *  clamped; total function; 0 → 1 → 0. */
export function travelSwing(progress: number): number;
```

Camera z: `z(p) = 12 + 78 · travelSwing(p)`; backdrop scale: `S(p) = 1 + 7 · travelSwing(p)` — both peaking exactly at the midpoint and landing exactly on the rest values (continuity at p = 0.5 asserted: 90 and 8 from both branches). Pinned tables (all dyadic-exact):

| p | camera z | starfield scale | | p | camera z | starfield scale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 12 | `(1, 1, 1)` | | 0.625 | 80.25 | `(7.125, 7.125, 1)` |
| 0.125 | 21.75 | `(1.875, 1.875, 1)` | | 0.75 | 51 | `(4.5, 4.5, 1)` |
| 0.25 | 51 | `(4.5, 4.5, 1)` | | 0.875 | 21.75 | `(1.875, 1.875, 1)` |
| 0.375 | 80.25 | `(7.125, 7.125, 1)` | | 1 | 12 | `(1, 1, 1)` |
| 0.5 | 90 | `(8, 8, 1)` | | | | |

The scene's `levelChanged` handling (dispose old cube view, build the new one in the new style) is unchanged code — only the moment it fires moves. The camera keeps looking at the origin (identity quaternion at every p).

**Reduced motion (explicit).** Under `prefers-reduced-motion`, the arrival **collapses to the predecessor's instant swap**: `travelMs = 150`, the camera stays at rest, the scale stays `(1,1,1)`, the level still advances at the midpoint of the 150 ms, and the new cube appears at the rest pose — the documented collapse, consistent with the AC-12 philosophy (no flights, no expansion).

**Engine semantics (unchanged except the timing).** `levelIndex` advances, a fresh board is built, the next style is applied (id differs; cycles in order), orientation resets to default, `stats` carry over, exactly one `levelChanged` intent drains, `travelStarted` drains once at travel start, the phase returns to `playing` at `travelMs`, and no input is accepted during `travel`. The level advance fires exactly once per travel: on the first `advance(dtMs > 0)` where `phaseElapsedMs` crosses `travelMs · TRAVEL_LEVEL_ADVANCE_P` (a single `advance` that crosses both the midpoint and the end fires the level advance first, then the phase switch; a zero midpoint fires immediately). `dtMs ≤ 0` remains a no-op.

## User stories

- As a player, I want the starry backdrop to fill the whole viewport and never develop an empty side, however long I watch, so the sky always looks alive.
- As a player, I want the intro to show me the cube immediately — far away, dead ahead — and approach it smoothly, so entering the game reads as flying toward my goal, not searching for it.
- As a player, I want the level change to fly me to the next cube — depart, see the new cube appear in the distance, fly in, and only then play — so the transition feels like a journey, not a pop.
- As a player who prefers reduced motion, I want the same collapses as before — no flights, no expansion, and an instant level swap.

## Open questions

- **Q-1 — Supported aspect range.** Pinned `[2/3, 7/3]` for this feature's guarantees (backdrop coverage *and* cube framing). **Recommended: keep.** Note honestly: at aspects below ≈ 0.564 (e.g., a phone in 9:16 portrait) the cube's nearest corner can touch the horizontal frustum edge at the closest approach — a pre-existing property of the accepted rest pose `(0,0,12)` + FOV 50, not introduced here; the backdrop cone would still cover, and the cube stays centred. Widening the guaranteed range below 0.564 needs the rest pose ≥ ≈ 12.031 (a supersession of the accepted AC-22 rest value — user checkpoint) or a wider FOV.
- **Q-2 — Intro start distance.** Pinned `INTRO_START_Z = 60` — the original accepted AC-22 value ("far away"), now with the always-pointing guarantee. **Recommended: keep.** A larger start (e.g., 90) is a constant-only change with a spec-revision note.
- **Q-3 — Arrival geometry.** Pinned: turn-around at `(0,0,90)` (the retained `TRAVEL_END_Z`), level advance at `p = 0.5`, symmetric `easeInOutQuad` halves, expansion peak 8 contracting back to 1. **Recommended: keep.** Alternatives (turn-around at 51, advance at 0.6, different easings) are pinned-constant changes, never silent.
- **Q-4 — Star density at the travel peak.** The expansion spreads the stars, so at the turn-around (scale 8) roughly 5 stars (square cone, 16:9; the builder's measured scale-8 count gets pinned exactly in the AC-1 test) are visible before the contraction condenses them back — the accepted expansion look (the predecessor's peak was comparably sparse), held only briefly. **Recommended: keep.** Alternatives: raise `STAR_COUNT` or lower `TRAVEL_STARFIELD_SCALE` — both supersede accepted constants and need the user checkpoint.
- **Q-5 — Reduced-motion arrival.** Pinned: collapses to the predecessor's **instant swap** (150 ms, camera at rest, no expansion, cube appears at rest). **Recommended: keep** — consistent with the AC-12 collapse philosophy (no flights under reduced motion).

## Assumptions

- "Background looks broken, on right side, there's no stars" = the landed starfield's slow **world-y-axis drift rotating its bounded disk**, which after ≈ 30–50 s of cumulative time pushes the disk's projected right boundary inside the viewport (plus an aspect-dependent depth shortfall at ≥ 21:9 even unrotated). Root cause: a bounded planar-ish distribution + a rigid yaw drift — a distribution that does not cover the visible frame. Fixed by the camera-anchored cone + the view-axis swirl (AC-1); the expansion scale itself was never the cause (it is symmetric about the screen centre).
- "Intro should be pointing to the cube BUT, the camera, being far away and, we, as the user, approaching to it" = a straight, eased dolly-in from `(0,0,60)` to `(0,0,12)` that keeps the cube centred and fully framed at every instant — no search/find segments (AC-2). The accepted AC-22 original values are reinstated with the pointing guarantee.
- "We should go to the next cube, not appearing this new cube instantly" = inside the existing `travel` phase: departure, the next cube appears at the turn-around distance (90 units), the camera flies back in, `playing` begins on landing — no new phase, no engine phase-machine change (AC-3).
- The user's expectations are directional, not numeric: the concrete values above (start distance, turn-around depth, midpoint timing, aspect range, margin) are this spec's pinned choices — Q-1…Q-5 carry them to the checkpoint.
- The starfield's swirl continues under reduced motion: it is the predecessor's ambient drift (never collapsed), relocated to the view axis; it is not a motion-triggering effect.
- The coverage guarantee holds for the pinned camera poses (all on the +z axis looking at the origin — true for every pose this feature and the accepted features define); a future oblique camera pose would need the anchor to carry the orientation (the anchor interface already copies the full quaternion, so only the guarantee statement, not the mechanism, would change).
- The accepted features' specs/plans/tasks stay authoritative for everything not superseded; this spec is read alongside them and never edits them.
- Stack unchanged: `three@0.186.1` / `@types/three@0.186.0`, TypeScript strict, Vitest, ESLint; the project testing bar (concrete assertions, no banned matchers, ≥ 80 × 4 per workspace) governs.
